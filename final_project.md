### Project Overview

For this project, you will design and conduct an experiment investigating the performance of computer hardware, technology, or an architectural feature.

You will select a topic, develop a research question, predict the expected results, conduct performance tests, and analyze your findings using concepts studied in this course.

The goal is to **apply computer architecture concepts to real-world performance**, not simply determine which computer or technology is faster.

Your experiment should be manageable using hardware and software available to you. Discuss your topic and experimental approach with your instructor before beginning. No formal proposal is required.

### 1. Select a Topic and Design an Experiment

Choose a topic that allows you to compare performance under at least two different conditions.

Possible topics include:

- Compare application performance on computers with different CPUs or GPUs.
- Compare data transfer speeds using different storage devices or connections (USB-A, USB-C, SSD, etc.).
- Investigate how available RAM affects application performance.
- Compare wired versus wireless network performance.
- Compare processing large amounts of data in memory versus using storage.
- Investigate single-core versus multicore processing performance.

Other topics may be selected with instructor approval.

**Experimental Requirements:**

- Develop a specific research question related to computer performance.
- Form a hypothesis predicting the results based on computer architecture concepts.
- Compare at least **two testing conditions**.
- Conduct at least **three trials for each condition**.
- Keep other variables as consistent as possible between tests.
- Collect measurable performance data, such as execution time, transfer speed, or resource utilization.

### 2. Written Report (3–5 Pages)

Prepare a **3–5 page report**, including graphs and tables, organized using the following sections.

**A. Introduction and Technology Overview**

- Describe the hardware or technology you are investigating.
- Explain how the technology works and its relationship to computer architecture.
- State your specific research question.

**B. Expected Results (Hypothesis)**

- Describe your prediction about the performance differences.
- Explain why you expect these results based on computer architecture concepts.

**C. Experimental Methodology**

- Identify the hardware and software used, including relevant specifications.
- Describe the testing conditions being compared.
- Explain the steps used to conduct your experiment.
- Identify what performance measurements were collected.
- Provide sufficient detail for someone else to repeat your experiment.

**D. Results**

- Present the individual measurements from at least three trials for each testing condition.
- Calculate and report the average results for each condition.
- Include a table showing your measurements and at least one graph comparing performance.
- Include evidence that you conducted the experiment, such as screenshots of benchmark results, photographs of your setup, or captured program output.
- Briefly describe the major findings.

**E. Analysis and Conclusions**

- Compare your actual results with your original hypothesis.
- Explain the results using computer architecture concepts.
- Discuss possible reasons for unexpected results or differences between trials.
- Identify at least one limitation or factor that may have influenced your results.
- Summarize what you learned from your experiment.

**References**

Include at least **two relevant references** supporting your technology overview, prediction, or analysis. Cite references within your report using a consistent citation style (APA, MLA, etc.).

References and additional supporting evidence in appendices do not count toward the 3–5 page requirement.

Include your name, project title, and date at the beginning of your report.

### 3. Research Poster

Create an **11 × 14-inch research poster** summarizing your experiment. Use the PowerPoint poster template provided on the course website.

Your poster must include:

- References.

Your poster should emphasize the most important information and use visual elements rather than lengthy paragraphs.

The poster will be projected on a TV screen during your presentation. **You do not need to print your poster.** Posters may also be printed by the department for display in the Science Hall.

Briefly acknowledge any substantive use of AI tools in your report.
